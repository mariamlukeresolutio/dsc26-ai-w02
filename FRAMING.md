## Decision
The model supports the fraud team in deciding which instant transfers should be reviewed for possible mule activity. Dina's team has limited analyst capacity, so only the highest-risk cases should be escalated for manual investigation. A false negative may allow fraudulent activity to continue, while a false positive increases analyst workload and review costs.

## Metric
Precision and recall are more useful than accuracy for this problem. The chosen threshold should balance catching as many mule cases as possible while keeping the number of alerts manageable for the analyst team. Threshold selection should therefore be based on expected business cost and analyst capacity rather than overall accuracy.

## Baseline
Without a model, analysts must rely on manual reviews or simple rules that may miss suspicious transfers or generate too many alerts. A useful baseline is the current review process or a simple rule-based approach. The scoring system should produce better prioritization and lower investigation cost than those alternatives.

## Classical ML or LLM
This is a structured tabular classification problem with transaction-level features and historical labels. Classical machine learning is a better fit than an LLM because the objective is to predict risk from structured data and optimize decision thresholds. LLMs may assist with reporting or investigations, but the risk score itself should be generated by a classical machine learning approach.
